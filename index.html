Production KPIs, out of Excel

A manufacturing plant tracked its production KPIs in an Excel workbook that one person updated by hand, so yield, cycle time and production reached the shift meeting late and depended on that one person.

I moved them into Power BI: DAX measures for yield, cycle time and production, a scheduled refresh that finished before each shift meeting, and views by shift, day and product with the target drawn on every trend. The production team got one report that kept itself up to date.

A 1.5 GB model brought down to 0.9 GB

A commercial reporting model had grown to 1.5 GB. Refreshing it took 40 minutes, and it was slow to open and hard to change.

I measured how much space each column took, removed the ones no visual or measure used, simplified the model around its relationships and rewrote the heaviest DAX measures. The model ended at 0.9 GB and refresh dropped to 20 minutes.

One procurement source on Microsoft Fabric

Procurement reports each pulled SAP HANA data on their own, with their own logic, and the large SAP tables made every refresh heavy.

I brought the SAP tables in with Dataflow Gen2, landed the large ones in a Lakehouse first, shaped them into a Warehouse, and put a single semantic model on top. Ten SAP tables ended up as one governed source for procurement reporting, and no report queries SAP directly anymore.

Weekly sales report, from five hours to one

A sales team rebuilt its weekly report by hand, which took about five hours every week, and every region could see everyone else's numbers.

I set up an automated ETL feeding an interactive Power BI report, added row-level security so each region saw only its own accounts, and ran short sessions with the team so they could answer their own questions. Preparation dropped to about an hour, which freed roughly 20 hours a month for customer work. Lead conversion rose 10% over the same period.
